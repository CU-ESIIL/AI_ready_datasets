# Unsupported claims after the transfer study

- Real-agent behavior, metadata efficacy, ecological analogs or real-fire prediction.
- A new independent hidden cohort: M4 Task A test performance was already observed.
- Statistically independent tasks: B–E share subsequent observations and are correlated.
- General scientific sufficiency of a representation from a few predictive heads.
- Exact information absence from failure of a linear probe, or exact invertibility
  from successful nonlinear prediction.
- Learned completeness detection from an all-complete validated input cohort.
- Physical causal acceleration or mechanisms from the future-versus-past mean criterion.
- Zero-shot transfer: B–E lightweight heads use training outcomes; original encoder
  weights alone remain frozen. E-specific encoders explicitly receive E supervision.
- A-only transfer credit for gains produced by additional A–D or task-specific training.
- Precisely equal capacities/regularization across all architectures or probe classes.
- Ecological relevance of top-ten nearest future log-increment vectors.
- Independent samples from same-fire prefixes, repeated initializations or retrieval
  queries/candidates; population wildfire significance from conditional fire bootstraps.
- Predictive robustness to unsupported missing/changed-resolution schedules merely
  because a source contract rejects them.
- A scientific acceptance threshold selected after the results, or proof that a
  task-specific encoder is mathematically necessary because it wins one comparison.
- Universal cost advantage from shared computation or stored intermediate vectors.
- Measured complete lifecycle costs, refresh frequency, storage-service pricing,
  review/maintenance labor or heterogeneous future-head reuse outside the tested bundles.

All retained observations are explicitly synthetic. No external model inference,
new real-source rows, source license inference or production execution approval.
